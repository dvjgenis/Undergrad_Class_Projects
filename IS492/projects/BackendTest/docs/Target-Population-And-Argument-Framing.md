# Target Population & Argument Framing

**Date:** March 4, 2025  
**Purpose:** Reposition the HarmonyForge argument to focus on the populations who will actually use the system. Use this framing when drafting the Overleaf manuscript (Introduction, Problem Statement, "Who Cares").

---

## 1. Target Populations (Stakeholder Personas)

HarmonyForge is designed for **three distinct user groups** identified through thematic analysis. The argument must center on these audiences; avoid broad "musicians" or "creators" language.

### 1.1 Gigging Musicians
- **Who:** Semi-professional or professional performers
- **Need:** Rapid, reliable arrangements for "Random Ensembles" and ad-hoc jam sessions
- **Pain:** "Time-Crunch" scenarios; manual transcription and clef transposition consume hours or weeks
- **Barrier:** "Random Ensemble" — e.g., violin must substitute for missing cello; repertoire must adapt quickly
- **Value proposition:** Shifts orchestration from tedious bottleneck to efficient co-creative process; allows ad-hoc instrument inclusion

### 1.2 Music Educators & Students
- **Who:** Teachers and learners using the tool for pedagogy
- **Need:** "Theory Inspector" as scaffolding partner; explicit, explainable feedback
- **Pain:** Gap between innate musical sense and explicit theory; Black Box AI cannot teach
- **Value proposition:** "Glass Box" interpretability bridges theory and intuition; Red Line validation acts as pedagogical proofreader

### 1.3 Chamber Ensemble Coordinators
- **Who:** Users responsible for repertoire expansion and instrumentation doubling
- **Need:** Reliable arrangements for non-standard lineups; confidence in theoretical validity
- **Pain:** Administrative labor (transcription, transposition) as prohibitive barrier to repertoire expansion
- **Value proposition:** Reduces "Mechanical Toil"; enables "Collective Jamming" and repertoire accessibility

---

## 2. Argument Framing (Do Not Use)

- **Too broad:** "Musicians," "creators," "music producers" — dilutes the specific workflows
- **Wrong focus:** Hobbyists, casual listeners, or purely generative AI users — they are not the primary adopters
- **Abstract only:** Framing the problem purely as "AI transparency" without tying it to these personas

---

## 3. Argument Framing (Use)

- **Gigging:** "For gigging musicians facing the Random Ensemble barrier, HarmonyForge..."
- **Educators:** "For music educators and students, the Theory Inspector serves as a pedagogical partner..."
- **Coordinators:** "For chamber ensemble coordinators managing repertoire expansion..."
- **Concrete scenarios:** "When a violinist must cover a cello part for tonight's gig..." or "When a teacher needs to explain why a parallel fifth sounds wrong..."

---

## 4. "Who Cares?" (Abstract/Introduction)

1. **Primary:** Gigging musicians and ensemble coordinators — flexibility of chamber groups; shift from technical bottleneck to co-creative process
2. **Secondary:** Music educators — pedagogical partner with Glass Box interpretability
3. **Broader AI community:** Copyright Safety by Design; rule-based core vs. Lakh MIDI; legally resilient alternative for commercial adoption
4. **Core thesis:** Expressive Sovereignty over Black Box automation — users refocus energy from notation mechanics to performance artistry
