Abstract
What is the context of the work? [1] 
Current generative music AI is dominated by stochastic "Black Box" models, such as the Anticipatory Music Transformer (Thickstun et al., 2024) and DeepBach (Hadjeres et al., 2017), which prioritize probabilistic texture over structural integrity. While these models excel at surface-level mimicry, they suffer from "structural drift"—a phenomenon where probabilistic "guessing" leads to theoretically invalid hallucinations (e.g., parallel fifths or voice-crossing) that violate the rigid harmonic rules of professional chamber music. For the gigging musician, these systems create a "Mechanical Toil" bottleneck; because the output is often unquantized MIDI or fixed audio, users are forced into a strenuous, multi-hour repair phase to ensure the arrangement is performable and "Musically Honest." Consequently, despite the speed of AI generation, the lack of tactile agency and symbolic editability prevents these tools from being integrated into real-world professional workflows.
What’s the gap?
Recent research identifies a critical "Collaging and Refinement" phase in AI-assisted creativity, in which users must integrate AI-generated fragments into their own work to maintain Expressive Sovereignty. However, a profound "Workflow Gap" exists because current tools provide "all-or-nothing" automation rather than a collaborative environment. For professional musicians, this results in a "Repair Phase Bottleneck": the time saved by AI generation is immediately lost to the cognitive load of "forced auditing"—manually hunting for subtle harmonic errors in an opaque system. Furthermore, as noted by Louie et al. (2020), users feel a diminished sense of ownership when AI does too much. There is a lack of a "Jumping Pad"—a system that respects the Latency-Utility Threshold (preserving the user's train of thought) while providing the "Glass Box" transparency needed for the user to remain the primary creative steward.
How do you address this? – new, (method)
We propose HarmonyForge, a three-stage system that replaces probabilistic "guessing" with an Axiomatic Hierarchy:
Generation (The Logic Core): Drawing on the "Glass Box" philosophy of MusicAIR (Liao et al., 2025) and HarmonySolver (Pawłowski et al., 2020), we implement a deterministic constraint-satisfaction engine. Unlike the probabilistic grammars of SchenkComposer (Hahn et al., 2023), our engine uses a strictly rule-based backtracking solver to guarantee theoretically valid SATB foundations.
Repair/Refinement (The Tactile Sandbox): We provide a direct-manipulation web interface where users exercise "Edit-Authority." Musicians can drag notes and modify inversions, transitioning the workflow from "passive recipient" to " active Creative Stewardship."
Explainability (The Theory Inspector): Following the "Reviewer Agent" pattern of ComposerX (Deng et al., 2024), we embed an LLM-driven "Theory Tutor." Rather than suggesting notes (which LLMs do poorly), the system acts as a "Theory Spell-checker," providing visual "Red Line" validation and ante-hoc explanations of theoretical conflicts found in user edits, as justified by Zhou et al. (2024).
What do you expect to find?
We hypothesize that this "Glass Box" approach will significantly outperform end-to-end deep learning models in "Repair Efficiency" by reducing the time spent on "forced auditing." By automating the "Mechanical Toil" of clef and key transposition, the system will achieve "Train-of-Thought Preservation," keeping users below the critical 4-minute latency-utility threshold. Furthermore, we expect to see a marked increase in Perceived Agency; as suggested by Tchemeube et al. (2025), the ability to override deterministic logic with "Human Flair" will lead to greater user ownership than purely generative tools. Finally, by providing visual "Red Line" validation, we aim to demonstrate that "Musically Honest" determinism builds a more resilient form of user trust—shifting the musician’s role from a skeptical proofreader to a confident co-creator.
Who cares?
This research addresses the critical scarcity of personalized, editable repertoire that currently limits the flexibility of chamber groups. For gigging musicians and ensemble coordinators, HarmonyForge shifts the orchestration workflow from a tedious technical bottleneck to an efficient, co-creative process—resolving the "Random Ensemble" barrier by allowing for ad-hoc instrument inclusion (e.g., transcribing a piano theme for a violin/cello duo).
Beyond utility, this system serves as a pedagogical partner for music educators, using "Glass Box" interpretability to bridge the gap between innate musical sense and explicit theory. For the broader AI community, this work provides a blueprint for "Copyright Safety by Design"; by using a rule-based core rather than training on the copyrighted datasets used by "Goliaths" like the Lakh MIDI dataset (Thickstun et al., 2024), we offer a legally resilient alternative for commercial adoption. Ultimately, by prioritizing Expressive Sovereignty over black-box automation, HarmonyForge empowers users to refocus their energy from the mechanics of notation to the artistry of performance.

2. Background and Motivation
What is the problem you are going to address?
The "Gigging Musician" Bottleneck & Mechanical Toil: Professional chamber groups and band ensembles frequently operate under significant "Time-Crunch" scenarios. Although these groups often require arrangements for ad-hoc instrumentation—such as a "Random Ensemble" where a violin must substitute for a missing cello—current manual workflows necessitate substantial "Mechanical Toil". This process involves strenuous, non-creative tasks like manual transcription and clef transposition, which can consume hours or even weeks, depending on complexity. Consequently, this administrative labor functions as a prohibitive barrier to repertoire expansion and collective "jamming".
The Reliability Crisis in Generative AI: Current SOTA probabilistic models, such as the Anticipatory Music Transformer (Thickstun et al., 2024), fail this user group in three ways:
Structural Drift: They "guess" harmonies, frequently hallucinating parallel fifths or voice-leading errors that break the professional "polish" required for performance.
The "Forced Auditing" Loop: Because the logic is opaque, musicians must spend their limited time proofreading every note, negating the efficiency gains of AI.
Loss of Expressive Sovereignty: Users feel like "passive recipients" of an AI’s style. As noted by Louie et al. (2020), if the AI does too much without providing a "Jumping Pad" for human flair, the musician loses their sense of authorship.
Why is this topic important or interesting?
Bridging the "Workflow Gap" via Symbolic Editability: HarmonyForge prioritizes the "Collaging and Refinement" phase. By generating valid, editable MusicXML, we ensure that the user retains "Edit-Authority." This shifts the AI's role from a "Black Box" creator to a "Tactile Assistant" that preserves the user's "Train-of-Thought."
Establishing "Copyright Safety by Design": Unlike models trained on the Lakh MIDI dataset, which Thickstun et al. admit "risk infringing on copyrighted material," HarmonyForge is "Musically Honest." Its core is built on public-domain music theory axioms (e.g., Schenkerian analysis). This provides the "Copyright Ambiguity-Free" environment necessary for professional and commercial adoption.
Advancing "Glass Box" Explainability (XAI): Following the "Ante-hoc" transparency framework (Liu et al., 2025), we transform the AI into a "Pedagogical Partner." Through a "Theory Inspector" that provides visual "Red Line" validation, the system explains the "physics" of a chord. This fosters the trust required for high-stakes environments like classrooms or professional stages.

3. Research Question(s) or Objective(s)
Research Question(s): 
MainRQ: To what extent does replacing probabilistic generation with deterministic logic and explainable critique enhance musician agency and trust when arranging lead sheets for non-standard ensembles?
Sub-Questions:
RQ1: How do limitations in part availability affect musicians' participation and satisfaction in ensembles?
RQ2: What are the current manual practices and pain points for adapting music?
RQ3: How do musicians perceive the integration of algorithmic/AI tools in the arrangement process?
RQ4: What features and guardrails are required for a human-centric music arrangement tool?
What prior work or literature does it relate to?
HarmonyForge aligns with the architectural rigor of HarmonySolver (Pawłowski et al., 2020) and MusicAIR (Liao et al., 2025), adopting their purely algorithmic composition cores to ensure outputs are theoretically valid (SATB-compliant) and "Copyright Ambiguity-Free." While we adopt the top-down structural depth of SchenkComposer (Hahn et al., 2023), we evolve their framework by replacing Probabilistic Context-Free Grammars (PCFG) with deterministic axioms to eliminate the "aimless wandering" and hallucinations found in deep learning models.
We contrast our approach against probabilistic "Goliaths" like DeepBach (Hadjeres et al., 2017) and the Anticipatory Music Transformer (Thickstun et al., 2024). While these models represent the state-of-the-art in texture generation, they frequently suffer from "Structural Drift" and infringe on intellectual property by training on copyrighted datasets like Lakh MIDI. HarmonyForge serves as a foil to these "Black Boxes," prioritizing structural reliability for high-stakes professional and educational environments.
Our architecture leverages the finding by Zhou et al. (2024) that LLMs perform poorly as music creators but excel as music theorists. By implementing the "Reviewer Agent" pattern from ComposerX (Deng et al., 2024), we utilize the LLM solely as a Theory Inspector. This provides the "Ante-hoc" explainability defined by Liu et al. (2025), ensuring that the AI functions as a transparent pedagogical partner rather than an opaque oracle.
What is our role-modeling paper?
Our primary role-modeling paper is HarmonySolver by Pawłowski, Sikora, and Sroka (2020), which serves as both a technical and functional blueprint for HarmonyForge. Unlike contemporary neural models, HarmonySolver utilizes a symbolic, rule-based constraint satisfaction engine to solve functional harmony exercises within a strictly vertical SATB framework. We model our project after their "non-AI" deterministic approach because it guarantees the enforcement of pedagogical constraints—such as interval prohibitions and voice ranges—without the "harmonic hallucinations" or parallel fifths common in probabilistic systems. Crucially, HarmonySolver is our deployment role model; as one of the few symbolic research projects to successfully transition into a functional MuseScore Plugin, it proves that a logic engine can be integrated directly into a professional notation workflow. HarmonyForge builds upon this foundation by evolving the solver from a static pedagogical tool into a dynamic co-creative partner, incorporating the "Reviewer Agent" patterns from ComposerX (Deng et al., 2024) to add the conversational explainability that Pawłowski’s original system lacks.
What new perspective do you aim to discover, develop, or demonstrate?
We aim to demonstrate that in high-stakes creative domains like music arrangement, Generative AI is most effective when utilized as a critic and auditor rather than a primary creator. We contrast our "Axiomatic Hierarchy"—a logic-first, AI-second architecture—against the current industry standard of "Probabilistic Guessing." By operationalizing Schenkerian Analysis within a deterministic framework, we aim to prove that "Glass Box" transparency does not stifle creativity; rather, it augments it by relieving the musician of "Mechanical Toil." Our perspective suggests that user trust and agency are not derived from the AI's ability to mimic human patterns, but from its ability to provide a "Musically Honest" foundation that the user can then override with their own "Human Flair." Ultimately, we demonstrate that by shifting the AI's role from a black-box generator to an explainable "Pedagogical Partner," we can bridge the "Workflow Gap" and transform the tedious repair phase into a moment of intentional, co-creative stewardship.

4. Methodology / Approach
What methods or techniques will you use?
Phase I - Axiomatic Logic (Input & Generation): The system begins with the user uploading a lead sheet (melody and chords). Rather than using a transformer to "guess" the next notes, we employ a backtracking constraint satisfaction solver in TypeScript. This engine applies the deterministic music theory rules modeled after HarmonySolver (Pawłowski et al., 2020) to generate a theoretically valid SATB foundation. By utilizing the hierarchical "Global Planning" framework suggested by SchenkComposer (Hahn et al., 2023), the engine ensures the arrangement follows a logical harmonic syntax (T->D->T) before the user even begins the repair phase.
Phase II - The Tactile Sandbox (Manual Repair & Refinement): The generated score is rendered in an interactive, web-based notation editor powered by VexFlow or OpenSheetMusicDisplay. This phase facilitates "Direct Manipulation," where the user exercises "Edit-Authority" to drag notes, change inversions, or add non-chord tones. This directly addresses the "Collaging and Refinement" step found in the literature, allowing the musician to inject "Human Flair" and resolve the "stiff" nature of rule-based generation.
Phase III - LLM Co-Creation (The Theory Inspector & Dispatcher): Adopting the multi-agent "GroupChatManager" architecture from ComposerX (Deng et al., 2024), we implement a Group Leader to serve as the system’s intent classifier and orchestrator. Rather than driving generation, the Leader monitors the "Tactile Sandbox" for user inputs (both natural language and symbolic edits) and dynamically routes control to three specialized agents:
The Auditor (Automated Validation): Triggered by symbolic state changes (score deltas), the Leader assigns the Auditor to validate user edits against the deterministic Axiomatic Engine. Drawing on the constraint-satisfaction logic of HarmonySolver (Pawłowski et al., 2020), the Auditor flags theoretical violations (e.g., parallel fifths, tessitura breaches) using visual "Red Line" indicators, ensuring the score remains "Musically Honest" without overriding the user's "Edit-Authority."
The Tutor (Ante-hoc Explainability): When the Leader detects a pedagogical query (e.g., "Why is this note marked red?"), it activates the Tutor. Leveraging findings that LLMs excel at theoretical reasoning even when they struggle with generation (Zhou et al., 2024), the Tutor provides non-prescriptive, ante-hoc explanations of the underlying music theory rules, fostering the "Glass Box" transparency required for educational scaffolding.
The Stylist (Creative Translation): Triggered by high-level aesthetic prompts (e.g., "Make this cadence more Brahms-ian"), the Leader assigns the Stylist to translate semantic descriptors into candidate symbolic edits. As demonstrated in MuseCoco (Lu et al., 2023) and ComposerX, this agent bridges the gap between natural language and notation, offering specific modification options that the user can accept or reject, thereby maintaining "Expressive Sovereignty" (Louie et al., 2020).
What tools or platforms will be involved, if there is any?
Technical Stack & Infrastructure
Frontend & Interface: The application is built using Next.js (React) and Tailwind CSS to manage a responsive, dashboard-style UI. The core notation rendering and interactive "Tactile Sandbox" are powered by VexFlow, allowing for note-level "Direct Manipulation" and "Edit-Authority."
Logic Engine (Backend): The Axiomatic Core is a custom backtracking constraint solver written in Node.js (TypeScript). It processes MusicXML and MIDI data to enforce the deterministic SATB rules modeled after HarmonySolver.
Explainability Layer: We utilize the OpenAI API (GPT-4o) to power the "Theory Inspector." Following the "Reviewer Agent" pattern, the LLM analyzes JSON-based score deltas to provide ante-hoc explanations of theoretical conflicts.
Design & Prototyping: Figma and other vibe coding design software are used for high-fidelity prototyping, specifically focusing on the ergonomics of "Red Line" Validation and the “Theory Inspector” sidebar layout.
Evaluation & Validation Tools
Algorithmic Verification: We use the music21 (Python) toolkit to calculate the Harmonic Error Rate (HER). This serves as our "Ground Truth" for measuring the reliability of our engine against probabilistic models like DeepBach.
Performance Metrics: Custom telemetry will track the "Repair Efficiency" metric, measuring the time delta between generation and a "performance-ready" state to ensure we meet the Latency-Utility Threshold.
User Experience: We will deploy the Creativity Support Index (CSI), System Usability Scale (SUS), and Net Promoter Score (NPS) with a specific focus on the "Transparency" and "Agency" sub-scales to validate the "Glass Box" design.
What datasets or participants (if any) will be needed?
The "Axiomatic" Knowledge Base (Datasets): Rather than utilizing pattern-based datasets (like the Lakh MIDI corpus), our logic engine is informed by deterministic rule-sets extracted from foundational music theory pedagogy.
Axiomatic Logic Source: Rules are codified from established harmony textbooks, specifically the Targosz and Sikorski functional harmony frameworks (as modeled in the HarmonySolver lineage). These provide the "ground truth" for SATB constraints (e.g., voice ranges, interval prohibitions, and resolution of the leading tone).

What do you expect to find, build, or contribute?
Through this research, we intend to contribute a "Glass Box" Co-Creative System that fundamentally redefines the relationship between musicians and generative technology. How will you evaluate the outcomes (e.g., metrics, feedback, comparisons)?
The "Repair Efficiency" Metric: We will conduct a timed within-subjects study where participants reach a "performance-ready" state using HarmonyForge versus a standard baseline (e.g., a standard notation editor starting from a raw GPT-4 MIDI output). Success is defined by a significant reduction in the time spent on "forced auditing" and "Mechanical Toil."
Harmonic Error Rate (HER): Utilizing the music21 library, we will quantitatively measure theoretical validity. We expect the "Axiomatic Engine" to maintain a 0% rate of parallel fifths and voice-crossing errors, whereas the probabilistic baseline is expected to show significant "Structural Drift."
Creativity Support Index (CSI): Following Tchemeube et al. (2025), we will utilize the CSI to measure "Results Worth Effort." This allows us to evaluate if the tool provides a "Jumping Pad" that augments the "fun" and curiosity of arranging without the frustration of aimless AI generation.
Pedagogical Growth Assessment: For student participants, we will implement pre- and post-tests to determine if interacting with the "Theory Inspector" improved their ability to identify voice-leading errors manually, measuring the tool's effectiveness as a "Pedagogical Partner."
System Usability Scale (SUS): We will adapt the SUS to include a sub-scale for "Interpretability" and "Agency," specifically testing if the "Red Line" validation increases the user's trust in the system's logic.
Net Promoter Score (NPS): To assess professional viability beyond mere usability, we will measure the NPS to determine the likelihood of adoption within gigging and educational networks. Following the validation protocol of Hahn et al. (2023), who utilized "likeliness to recommend" to benchmark the viability of SchenkComposer, this metric serves as a proxy for "Workflow Utility." A positive NPS will empirically validate that HarmonyForge successfully transitions from a "novelty toy" to a robust solution for the "Time-Crunch" scenarios faced by ensemble leaders, confirming that the reduction in "Mechanical Toil" translates into real-world professional value.

Quantitative Analysis: Reliability & Efficiency
Harmonic Error Rate (HER) Benchmarking: We will perform a comparative statistical analysis between the outputs of the Axiomatic Engine and the Anticipatory Music Transformer (Thickstun et al., 2024). Using music21 (Python), we will conduct a t-test on the frequency of theoretically invalid notes (hallucinations) to prove the "Musically Honest" reliability of our core.
Efficiency Analysis: We will measure the "Repair Efficiency" metric—the total time from initial generation to a "performance-ready" MusicXML file. Qualitative Analysis: Agency & Trust
Thematic Coding (Zambrano et al., 2025 Protocol): We will use the Zambrano Protocol to analyze transcripts from "Think-Aloud" user studies. Following our established "Red-to-Green" refinement, we will perform deductive coding for constructs like "Edit-Authority," "Expressive Sovereignty," and "Deductive Trust."
Creativity Support Index (CSI) (Tchemeube et al., 2025): We will apply the CSI specifically to measure the "Results Worth Effort" sub-scale. This metric is directly applicable because it evaluates whether the co-creative interaction (the "Collaging" phase) feels like a "Jumping Pad" or a "Nagging" distraction.
Adapted System Usability Scale (SUS): While the standard SUS measures general usability, we will follow the methodology of Louie et al. (2020) by adding specific Likert-scale questions regarding "Authorship" and "Perceived Control." This allows us to double-check if the "Theory Inspector" successfully reduces automation bias without overwhelming the user.
Net Promoter Score (NPS) & Professional Viability: To assess the system's potential for adoption in real-world professional workflows, we will measure the Net Promoter Score. Following the validation protocol of Hahn et al. (2023), who utilized the "likeliness to recommend" metric (achieving a mean of 9.0/10) to benchmark the viability of the SchenkComposer system, we employ NPS as a proxy for "Workflow Utility." This metric serves to empirically distinguish HarmonyForge from mere "novelty" generative tools; a positive NPS will indicate that the system's reduction of "Mechanical Toil" and provision of "Glass Box" transparency are sufficient to motivate adoption among time-constrained ensemble leaders and educators.

8. Potential Risks
Potential Risks, Technical Hurdles, and Mitigation
The "Nagging" Problem & Creative Stifling: A major risk identified in Theme 3.1 of our analysis is that the LLM "Theory Inspector" may become a "Nagging" presence. If the system flags every minor theoretical conflict (like a parallel fifth) during the initial creative phase, it could cause "Information Overload" and kill the musician's creative flow.
Mitigation: We will implement "Mode Toggling." Users can switch between "Strict Mode" (where the Tutor flags every error for pedagogical purposes) and "Creative Mode" (where the Tutor only speaks when prompted or provides a high-level "vibe check" without interrupting the user's "Human Flair").
The "Robotic" Rigidity of Axiomatic Logic: Rule-based music generation is historically criticized for sounding "stiff" or "textbook-like" compared to the expressive, pattern-rich outputs of diffusion models. There is a risk that the initial "Axiomatic" foundation might be too boring to serve as a high-quality "Jumping Pad" for professionals.
Mitigation: We are integrating Timbre-Preserving Playback and an LLM "Stylist" feature. The AI won't just generate rules; it will suggest decorations (non-chord tones, rhythmic heterogeneity) that the user can manually "collage" onto the valid structure to add life to the arrangement.
Mathematical Complexity of Schenkerian Hierarchy: Implementing a full Schenkerian analysis engine—which involves recursive layers of musical "background" and "foreground"—is mathematically difficult to achieve in real-time within a web browser. Attempting a total implementation could lead to high latency, breaking our 4-minute Latency-Utility Threshold.
Mitigation: We will implement a "Schenkerian Lite" hierarchy. This focuses specifically on Cadential Planning (ensuring phrase beginnings and endings are structurally sound) while leaving the internal "foreground" decorations to the user’s tactile manipulation.
Technical "Nagging" from Latency: If the "LLM Council" takes too long to analyze the score deltas, the user's "Train-of-Thought Preservation" will be lost.
Mitigation: We are exploring LangGraph to optimize the "Reviewer Agent" workflow, ensuring that the delta-analysis only processes the measures the user has changed, rather than re-scanning the entire score.
